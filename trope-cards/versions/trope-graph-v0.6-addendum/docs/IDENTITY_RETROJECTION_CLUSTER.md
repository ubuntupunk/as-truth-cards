# Identity Retrospection Cluster

This cluster captures arguments in which a modern identity, political category, or national identity is projected backwards onto an ancient person or population.

## Core mechanism

`RETROSPECTIVE_IDENTITY`

A claim assigns a later identity/category to an earlier person or population and treats the assignment as if the historical category were continuous and self-evident.

## Secondary mechanisms

- `ANACHRONISM` — a later category is applied to an earlier period without establishing historical continuity of meaning.
- `ESSENTIALISM` — ancestry, identity, religion, nationality, or ethnicity is treated as a fixed essence that passes unchanged across historical periods.

## Methodological test

For every retrospective-identity claim, decompose:

1. **Subject** — who or what is being classified?
2. **Historical period** — when did the subject exist?
3. **Claimed identity** — what category is being assigned?
4. **Contemporary meaning** — what did that category mean at the time?
5. **Modern meaning** — what does it mean now?
6. **Continuity bridge** — what evidence connects the historical and modern meanings?
7. **Inference** — is the conclusion historical, genealogical, theological, political, or rhetorical?

The graph must not collapse these seven questions into one claim.

## Initial cluster

- `Jesus Is a Muslim`
- `Jesus Was a Palestinian`
- `Jesus Was a Zionist`
- `Canaanite Card`
- `The Land of the Children of Israel` — contextual source/reference node
- `Chronology` — contextual node

## Typical inference chain

```text
ancient subject/population
        ↓
ancient characteristic or ancestry
        ↓
claimed continuity
        ↓
modern identity/category
        ↓
modern political or theological implication
```

Each arrow is independently researchable.

## Editorial rule

A card may document that a retrospective claim exists without endorsing the claim or reducing it to a "falsehood" card. Where evidence is genuinely disputed, competing interpretations belong in `interpretations` / `claim_interpretations` rather than being resolved by card title or taxonomy.
