# Antisemitism & Anti-Zionism Trope Card Deck
*Working draft — editorially expanded and reorganised from the current draft and the original card inventory.*

## Editorial note

This deck works best if it distinguishes **a rhetorical trope**, **a disputed factual proposition**, and **a legitimate political or theological disagreement**. A card should not imply that every criticism of Israel, Zionism, Zionist institutions, or Jewish theology is antisemitic. The useful test is the structure of the claim: what is being asserted, what evidence is offered, whether the claim generalises from an individual or government to Jews as a collective, and whether a historical stereotype is being transferred to a new target.

The three axes are:

- **Tactic-Naming** — identifies a rhetorical move, independent of whether a particular factual claim is true or false.
- **Fact-Rebuttal** — makes a testable factual claim and supplies evidence or a documented counter-record.
- **Theological-Dispute** — rests substantially on a contested reading of scripture, doctrine, covenant, or religious terminology rather than an empirical proposition.

A fourth editorial tag is useful throughout the deck:

- **Contested / legitimate-debate** — included because the subject is frequently presented as an antisemitism claim, but the underlying proposition can also be argued in good faith. Such cards should describe the competing interpretations rather than treating disagreement itself as proof of antisemitism.

# SUIT ONE: Classic Tropes

## Shylock Card — Tactic

You have just pulled the Shylock card. It invokes Shakespeare’s moneylender to portray Jews as inherently deceptive in commerce, predatory lenders, or naturally associated with usury. The stereotype has a much older history than Shakespeare: medieval European restrictions on Jewish landholding and guild membership pushed many Jewish communities toward occupations that included moneylending, after which the resulting economic role was itself turned into a stereotype. The card works because it exposes the circularity: discriminatory institutions help create a social role, and the role is then cited as evidence of an alleged Jewish character trait.

**Improvement:** keep the historical mechanism; avoid saying that Jews were “funnelled into finance” without distinguishing different regions and periods.

## Mossad Agent Card — Tactic

You have just pulled the Mossad Agent card. An opponent is portrayed as a covert operative of Israeli intelligence, usually without evidence beyond Jewish identity, Israeli connections, or an expressed political position. The rhetorical effect is to transform ordinary participation in a debate into evidence of clandestine infiltration. It is structurally related to older “hidden hand” accusations in which Jews were imagined to exercise secret influence through institutions that were otherwise publicly visible.

**Card test:** ask whether there is evidence of actual intelligence activity. If not, the accusation is doing rhetorical work rather than reporting a fact.

## Zio Nazi Card — Tactic / Holocaust inversion

The Zio Nazi card draws a direct equivalence between Nazi Germany and Israel or Zionism. Criticism of Israeli conduct can be legitimate and can include historical analogies, but the card is triggered when the analogy becomes a rhetorical inversion of Jewish persecution — for example, when Jews or Zionists are cast as the historical equivalent of their persecutors or the Holocaust is repurposed to erase Jewish vulnerability.

The useful distinction is between **historical analogy** and **Holocaust inversion**. The latter does not merely criticise Israeli policy; it reverses the roles of persecuted and persecutor.

## World Domination Card — Tactic

You have just pulled the World Domination card. Jews or “Zionists” are said to secretly control banks, media, governments, universities, or other institutions as part of a coordinated hidden agenda. The modern conspiracy tradition is closely associated with the forged *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*, which presented fabricated “minutes” of an alleged Jewish conspiracy.

**Improvement:** separate the historical *Protocols* card below from this broader contemporary pattern. The former is a specific forgery; this card is the recurring conspiracy structure.

## Dual Loyalty Card — Tactic

The Dual Loyalty card suggests that Jews cannot be fully loyal citizens because their primary allegiance lies with Jews, Israel, or a supposed “Jewish agenda.” The move changes an individual's political preference or religious connection into evidence of collective disloyalty. The card should distinguish ordinary criticism of an individual's actual conflict of interest from the blanket presumption that Jewish identity itself creates divided citizenship.

## Blood Libel Card — Tactic / historical mutation

The historical blood libel accused Jews of murdering Christian children for ritual purposes. Its modern relevance is structural rather than literal: an old accusation that Jews derive pleasure from the suffering or death of children can reappear with “Zionists” substituted for “Jews.” The card should be used carefully: a documented allegation that Israeli forces killed children is not automatically blood libel. The trope appears when the claim shifts from a specific event or policy to a collective accusation that Jews or Zionists inherently desire or celebrate child death.

## Holocaust Denial / Distortion Card — Fact-Rebuttal

Holocaust denial rejects or radically minimises the Nazi genocide of European Jews. Holocaust distortion is broader: it can involve minimising scale, manipulating chronology, selectively comparing events, or using the Holocaust as a rhetorical device to invert the historical relationship between victim and perpetrator.

**Improvement:** keep denial and distortion together for gameplay, but define the distinction explicitly. A disputed analogy about Israel is not automatically Holocaust denial; the card is strongest where the historical record is being falsified or deliberately inverted.

## Talmud Card — Tactic / borderline Theological

The Talmud card treats the Talmud as a single binding operational manual and then presents isolated, decontextualised passages as proof of a hidden Jewish doctrine. The Talmud is instead a large corpus preserving rabbinic argument, disagreement and minority opinions. A quotation therefore requires attention to speaker, context, surrounding argument and later legal status.

**Card test:** “Is the quotation being presented as a binding teaching of Judaism when the source itself is recording a dispute?”

## Chosen People / Master Race Card — Tactic / Theological

The card recasts the Jewish theological concept of being “chosen” as equivalent to racial supremacy. In Jewish theology, chosenness is commonly framed in terms of covenant, obligation and responsibility rather than a claim to biological superiority. The trope becomes especially stark when a religious concept is translated into Nazi racial categories and then used as evidence that Judaism inherently teaches domination.

**Improvement:** label this explicitly as a theological dispute card as well as a trope card. The deck should not imply that every theological discussion of chosenness is antisemitic.

## Havaara / “Blood for Goods” Card — Fact-Rebuttal / Holocaust inversion

The card concerns the 1933 Haavara Agreement and later Jewish rescue negotiations, including the controversy surrounding Rudolf Kastner. The trope presents these events as proof that Zionists or Jewish leaders collaborated with Nazism. The evidence instead concerns desperate and highly controversial rescue arrangements under Nazi persecution, with difficult choices and competing Jewish political interests.

**Improvement:** use the historically standard spelling **Haavara** and give the card a short “what actually happened” chronology. Do not compress Haavara, the Kastner case and later rescue negotiations into one undifferentiated event.

## Elders of Zion Card — Fact-Rebuttal

The *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* is a fabricated conspiracy text, not a record of an actual Jewish organisation. It was assembled and circulated in the Russian Empire in the early twentieth century and later became one of the most influential modern antisemitic conspiracy texts.

**Improvement:** correct “under Napolean” to the historically relevant Russian context and distinguish the *Protocols* from the earlier European conspiracy traditions that preceded it. The card should identify it as a **forgery** first and a trope second.

## Jews Are Not White Card — Tactic / racial essentialism

This card should not simply assert the opposite proposition to “Jews are White.” Its subject is the use of racial classification to make a predetermined political argument. Jewish populations include Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Mizrahi, Ethiopian and other communities with different histories and social classifications. Statements that categorically define all Jews as one race flatten this diversity.

**Improvement:** make the card about **racial essentialism**, not about establishing one universally correct racial category.

# SUIT TWO: Zionism-Coded Tropes

## Goy Card — Theological / terminology

*Goy* is a Hebrew word meaning “nation” and is also used in the Hebrew Bible for Israel. Genesis 12:2, for example, describes Abraham's descendants becoming a *goy gadol*, a “great nation.” In later Jewish usage, *goy* can refer to a non-Jew, but the word's biblical range makes simplistic claims about its meaning misleading.

**Improvement:** remove the suggestion that anyone using the term critically is necessarily invoking Replacement Theology. The card should identify the linguistic move and then explain when the theological claim begins.

## Replacement Theology Card — Theological / contested

Replacement theology, or supersessionism, refers to theological positions in which the Church is understood to supersede or fulfil Israel's covenantal role. Different Christian traditions formulate the doctrine differently. Applying it to Jewish claims about land, identity or covenant can become politically consequential, but the card should distinguish theological disagreement from an antisemitic claim about Jews.

**Improvement:** avoid treating “Islamic replacement” as a single established doctrine. Islamic traditions contain multiple theological positions concerning earlier revelation, Jewish communities and the land.

## Apartheid Collaborators Card — Fact-Rebuttal / contested

The card challenges narratives that single out Israel as uniquely responsible for sustaining apartheid South Africa. The historical record includes substantial relationships between apartheid South Africa and multiple foreign governments and corporations, alongside Jewish South Africans who were prominent in the anti-apartheid movement.

**Improvement:** this should be a **comparative history card**, not a claim that Israel had no relationship with apartheid South Africa. The stronger formulation is: “Was Israel involved? Yes. Was it uniquely responsible? That requires a comparative evidentiary argument.” The separate 1987 severance card provides one part of that record.

## White, European Settlers Card — Fact-Rebuttal / racial essentialism

This card addresses claims that all Israeli Jews are simply white European settlers. Jewish Israelis have diverse geographic and historical origins, including large communities with roots in the Middle East and North Africa as well as Europe and Ethiopia.

**Improvement:** avoid “over half” unless a current demographic source is attached to the card. Better: state the diversity and provide a source note with the precise population definition used.

## Settler-Colonialism Card — Fact-Rebuttal / contested

Settler-colonialism is an established academic framework and is used by scholars to analyse Zionism and Israel. It should not be treated as inherently antisemitic. The card should instead identify the **argumentative move** when the framework is presented as though it resolves every historical question by definition — for example, when the complex history of Jewish indigeneity, continuous Jewish presence, immigration, Arab nationalism, Ottoman rule, British administration and the Holocaust is reduced to a single settler/indigenous binary.

**Improvement:** this is one of the cards that most needs a “contested” label. Its value is methodological: ask what the framework explains and what evidence it leaves out.

## Zionist-as-Slur Card — Tactic / contested

“Zionist” can be a straightforward political descriptor, and criticism of Zionism is not inherently antisemitic. The card is triggered when “Zionist” is used as a substitute for “Jew,” especially where Jewish people as a collective are attributed sinister characteristics or subjected to exclusion, harassment or dehumanisation.

**Improvement:** replace the current claim that the term “increasingly” functions as a codeword unless the card has a cited dataset. The stronger formulation is structural: **when a political label is used as a proxy for an ethnic or religious group, examine whether the underlying stereotype has merely been relabelled.**

## Litmus Test / Deplatforming Card — Tactic

A person's assumed position on Zionism is treated as a sufficient reason to exclude them, boycott them, deny them a platform or attack their reputation, without engaging the person's actual conduct or argument. The rhetorical move is **guilt by assumed affiliation**.

A useful regional example is the dispute surrounding Professor Adam Mendelsohn at UCT. UCT's own public material confirms his April 2026 inaugural lecture, “Where to for the Jews?”; allegations about subsequent statements or litigation should be sourced directly to the court record or the parties' filings rather than inferred from the lecture itself. citeturn0search1

## Platform / Institutional Control Card — Tactic

The claim is that “Zionist” or Jewish influence secretly controls a platform, university, donor board, government agency or piece of legislation. The relevant trope is not criticism of lobbying or institutional influence as such; it is the leap from documented influence by identified actors to a hidden collective Jewish or Zionist command structure.

**Card test:** identify the actual actor, evidence of influence, mechanism and decision. If those disappear and “the Zionists” become the unexplained agent, the conspiracy structure is present.

## River to Sea Card — Theological / contested

“From the river to the sea, Palestine will be free” has multiple interpretations. Supporters can use it to mean Palestinian freedom and equal rights; critics interpret it as implying the elimination of Israel. Historical use by Palestinian political movements and the context in which a speaker uses the phrase therefore matter.

The mirror-image maximalist concept of a Jewish state extending across the whole territory is also relevant. The card should therefore make **context** the central question rather than treating the slogan as having one universally agreed meaning.

**Improvement:** do not use the existence of a particular charter alone as proof of what every contemporary speaker means by the slogan.

## Weaponizing Antisemitism Card — Tactic / contested

The claim is that accusations of antisemitism are being deployed strategically to silence criticism of Israel. This is a legitimate subject for investigation, but it can itself become a rhetorical shield if it automatically dismisses every antisemitism allegation as bad faith.

The strongest version of the card asks: **Is the allegation supported by the content and context, or is “antisemitism” being invoked merely to terminate debate?**

## Jews Are White Card — Tactic / racial essentialism

The converse racial essentialism assigns all Jews a single “white” identity and uses that classification to settle political questions about indigeneity, power or victimhood. Jewish populations have diverse histories and are classified differently across societies.

**Improvement:** pair this card visually with “Jews Are Not White.” The pair demonstrates that the underlying rhetorical mechanism is not the chosen racial category but the insistence that a complex population must fit one political category.

## Jews Are Not Semites — Fact-Rebuttal / terminology

“Antisemitism” is a nineteenth-century European political term for anti-Jewish hostility; it was popularised by Wilhelm Marr in the 1870s and 1880s. The term is not a claim that Jews are the only people who are “Semitic” in a linguistic or historical sense. The modern term refers specifically to hostility toward Jews. citeturn0search2turn0search11

**Improvement:** replace “reclaimed term” with **“nineteenth-century political coinage.”** The term was coined/popularised by antisemites themselves; it was not originally a Jewish self-description.

# SUIT THREE: South Africa — Regional Cases

These cards should be the most tightly sourced cards in the deck. Each should carry a **case name, court, case number, date, procedural posture and current status**.

## Cape Union Mart Card — Fact-Rebuttal

The Western Cape High Court delivered judgment on 4 September 2026 in *Cape Union Mart International (Pty) Ltd and Another v Ockards and Others* [2026] ZAWCHC 477. The court granted a final interdict restraining specified claims that Cape Union Mart or its brands were associated with Israel, funding genocide or military conflict in Gaza, or that the applicants were killing or complicit in killing children. The judgment expressly did not prohibit boycott activity or public protest, and it required protestors to comply with the Regulation of Gatherings Act. citeturn0search0

**Important improvement:** do not reduce the judgment to “the court found there was no factual basis.” The judgment is more precise: the respondents failed to establish the relevant factual propositions with admissible evidence sufficient to support their defences. The court also criticised aspects of the applicants' requested relief as overbroad and noted conduct by Mr Krawitz that it considered unfortunate. citeturn0search0

**Status:** live if an appeal or leave application remains pending; update after the next procedural event.

## UCT Resolutions Card — Tactic / Fact-Rebuttal

In June 2024, UCT Council adopted resolutions concerning a boycott of Israel and the IHRA definition. The Mendelsohn litigation challenges aspects of the process and institutional governance. The card should focus on the **governance question** rather than treating the existence of the resolutions as evidence of antisemitism.

**Improvement:** separate the institutional-process claim from the substantive political debate about Israel/Palestine. Cite the actual resolutions and pleadings.

## SA Jews for a Free Palestine Card — Tactic

The card concerns an argument from identity: because a speaker or organisation is Jewish, criticism of its position is presented as incapable of being antisemitic. Identity can be relevant context, but it does not logically determine whether a proposition is antisemitic.

**Improvement:** avoid framing this as a general rule about the organisation. Identify the exact statement, speaker, date and procedural status. The card should demonstrate the tactic through evidence rather than infer motive.

## Mendelsohn Card — Regional case

This card should be the **case-study hub** rather than repeating the Litmus Test card. It should contain:

1. who Professor Adam Mendelsohn is and his UCT role;
2. the 29 April 2026 inaugural lecture;
3. the UCT resolutions;
4. the statements or conduct complained of;
5. the causes of action actually pleaded;
6. the respondents' defence;
7. the court/procedural history;
8. what remains unresolved.

UCT's public notice confirms the inaugural lecture date and title. citeturn0search1

**Improvement:** do not label any disputed allegation as fact until it is supported by a judgment or clearly attributed to a pleading, affidavit or party.

# SUIT FOUR: Fact-Rebuttal Reference Cards

These are evidence cards rather than necessarily “tropes.” Their purpose is to supply the factual context needed when a trope card is drawn.

## Jerusalem Sovereignty Card

Claims about East Jerusalem should distinguish **historical administration**, **sovereignty**, and **present-day international legal status**. Jordan controlled East Jerusalem from 1948 until 1967, while Israel has exercised control since 1967. The card should not collapse those facts into a conclusion about present sovereignty.

**Improvement:** add a three-line chronology: 1948–1967 Jordanian administration; 1967 Israeli control; subsequent competing sovereignty claims and international positions.

## Quran / Land Claim Card — Theological / contested

The card can note that Islamic scripture contains passages concerning the Children of Israel and a blessed/holy land, but it should not claim that the Quran therefore establishes modern exclusive territorial sovereignty for Jews or Muslims. Scriptural description and modern international sovereignty are different categories.

**Improvement:** quote the relevant verses accurately and provide translation/version information. This will make the card considerably harder to dismiss as selective quotation.

## The “Apartheid Map” Card

The popular sequence-of-maps format should be tested against the actual legal and political status of each territory at each date. Maps can conflate land ownership, administrative control, proposed partition, armistice lines, military occupation and sovereign annexation.

**Improvement:** make the card about **map-category errors**, not simply “the map is false.” A companion evidence card should show the same dates with a legend explaining what each colour actually represents.

## Voting Rights Card

The card should distinguish Israeli citizens from residents of the West Bank and Gaza and distinguish national elections from Palestinian Authority elections. Arab citizens of Israel participate in Israeli elections; Palestinian residents of territories under Palestinian administration have a different political and legal status.

**Improvement:** use the precise election and jurisdiction being discussed. Avoid the blanket phrase “Arabs cannot vote.”

## Nakba Symmetry Card

The card compares the displacement of Palestinians in 1947–49 with the displacement and migration of Jewish populations from Arab and Muslim-majority countries during the same broad historical period.

**Improvement:** do not imply that the two histories are identical. The point is comparative completeness: if one population movement is used as evidence for a historical argument, the other should not disappear from the account.

## Balfour / Colonial Origins Card

A serious historical card should place the Balfour Declaration inside the larger diplomatic sequence rather than treating it as a solitary act that created Israel. Include the McMahon–Hussein correspondence, Sykes–Picot, the Ottoman surrender and post-war mandates, the Faisal–Weizmann episode, British policy and later UN partition proposals.

**Improvement:** make this a chronology card with dates, documents and disputed interpretations rather than a single “gotcha.”

## Palestinian Flag Card

The modern Palestinian flag belongs to the broader Pan-Arab flag tradition and is historically related to the Arab Revolt flag. The card should not imply that this genealogy invalidates Palestinian national identity; flags routinely inherit symbols from wider political movements.

**Improvement:** distinguish **origin of the design** from **meaning acquired by later Palestinian nationalism**.

## Chronology Card

The First Zionist Congress took place in 1897. Palestinian Arab nationalism developed through several overlapping late-Ottoman, Arab-nationalist, local and post-World War I political movements.

**Improvement:** avoid the unsupported claim that organised Palestinian nationalism simply began “three decades later.” The stronger card is about **competing national movements developing on different timelines**, which is historically more defensible and more useful.

## ANC / Hamas Equivalence Card

The card should distinguish the ANC's historical relationship with the PLO and Arafat from the later relationship between South African political actors and Hamas. Mandela's 1990 statement concerning Israel's right to exist is relevant evidence, but it should not be used to imply that the ANC's entire later policy is frozen at that date.

**Improvement:** make the card a timeline rather than a single quotation.

## Israel–Apartheid Severance Card

Israel's relationship with apartheid South Africa changed over time. The 1987 severance of remaining military ties and Shimon Peres's public denunciation of apartheid are important evidence, but they do not erase earlier Israeli-South African military and economic relationships.

**Improvement:** pair this card with Apartheid Collaborators so that the deck presents the whole chronology rather than selecting one convenient period.

## False Binary / “Choose Sides” Card — Tactic

The move demands that an observer choose one collective side and treats neutrality, qualification or criticism of both sides as moral failure. The card should distinguish this rhetorical pressure from situations where a specific legal or factual question genuinely has a binary answer.

**Card test:** “Does choosing a side replace examination of the particular claim?”

## Depo-Provera Card — Fact-Rebuttal / contested — use with care

This is one of the deck's most important safeguards against overreach. The documented issue concerns disproportionate administration of Depo-Provera and informed-consent failures affecting Ethiopian-Israeli women. That does not by itself establish a deliberate state policy of ethnic population suppression or genocide.

The card should explicitly separate:
1. **documented medical-ethics failures**;
2. **allegations of coercion**;
3. **claims about state intent**; and
4. **claims about a systematic genocidal programme**.

**Improvement:** attach primary-source citations and retain the “contested” flag. This card should demonstrate that the deck is willing to acknowledge adverse evidence rather than classify every counterargument as a trope.

## IHRA Isn’t Valid Card — Definition / institutional dispute

This should be reframed. The IHRA Working Definition is a non-legally binding definition adopted by the IHRA in 2016. It defines antisemitism as a certain perception of Jews that may be expressed as hatred and includes examples, several concerning Israel. citeturn0search6

The Jerusalem Declaration on Antisemitism was subsequently developed as an alternative framework. It argues that the IHRA definition is unclear in important respects and proposes a different core definition and contextual guidelines, while expressly stating that determining that a particular view is not antisemitic does not amount to endorsing that view. citeturn0search5

**Improvement:** rename the card **IHRA vs Jerusalem Declaration**. “IHRA Isn’t Valid” is too categorical and makes the card itself look like an unsupported verdict. The real subject is a documented dispute over definitions, scope, examples and institutional use.

# Cards still worth considering

The present deck is much more complete than the original 15-card inventory, but several **structural gaps** remain.

### 1. Guilt-by-Association Card
A person is treated as guilty because they attended an event, signed a letter, followed an organisation, accepted funding, or associated with someone described as a Zionist. This is broader than deplatforming and deserves its own tactic card.

### 2. Collective Responsibility Card
An individual Jew or Israeli is held responsible for actions of the Israeli state, Israeli military or another Jewish/Israeli institution solely because of identity. This is distinct from Dual Loyalty.

### 3. Jewish Lobby / Money Power Card
This would bridge the Classic “World Domination” card and the contemporary “Platform Control” card. The distinctive move is attributing political influence to a monolithic Jewish financial network rather than to identifiable organisations, donors, voters or interest groups.

### 4. Demonisation / Dehumanisation Card
A general tactic card for language that describes Jews, Israelis or Zionists as vermin, disease, parasites, inherently evil, or collectively beyond redemption. This provides a bridge between classic antisemitic imagery and contemporary political rhetoric.

### 5. From Individual to Collective Card
A factual allegation against an Israeli official, soldier, settler or organisation is generalised to all Jews or all Zionists. This is a useful “logic card” because it identifies the inferential error rather than debating the underlying event.

### 6. Nazi Germany / Zionism “Secret Alliance” Card
This could be separated from Havaara if the deck repeatedly encounters the broader claim that Zionism and Nazism were ideologically allied. Haavara is one historical episode; the “secret alliance” claim is a much broader proposition.

### 7. Apartheid Definition Card
A particularly important missing **counterweight**. Instead of asking whether Israel “is apartheid,” define the legal concept of apartheid and then identify the evidence and competing legal analyses. This prevents the deck from treating the label itself as the argument.

### 8. Genocide Definition Card
Likewise, separate the legal definition of genocide from political usage of the word. This would be especially useful alongside the Cape Union Mart card, where the court record concerns allegations of genocide against a retailer and individual rather than adjudicating the Gaza war itself.

### 9. Antisemitism vs Anti-Zionism Card
A foundational taxonomy card. It should show:
- criticism of Israeli government policy;
- opposition to Zionism;
- criticism of a specific Zionist organisation;
- criticism that invokes classic anti-Jewish stereotypes;
- discrimination against Jews because they are Jews.

The categories overlap in some cases but are not identical.

### 10. Jewish / Israeli / Zionist Identity Card
Another foundational taxonomy card. These terms should never be treated as synonyms. The deck currently relies on this distinction but does not yet give the reader a dedicated reference card for it.

# Recommended architecture

The deck will become substantially stronger if every card carries the same compact metadata:

**CARD NAME**  
**Suit:** Classic / Zionism-coded / South Africa / Reference  
**Axis:** Tactic / Fact-Rebuttal / Theological / Contested  
**Trigger:** one sentence describing what causes the card to be drawn  
**Mechanism:** what rhetorical or factual move is occurring  
**Counter-test:** the question a reader should ask before accepting the card  
**Evidence:** primary or authoritative sources  
**Status:** settled / disputed / live litigation / update required

This will stop the deck from becoming a collection of essays and turn it into a repeatable analytical system.

## Source hierarchy

For the final publication version, use:

1. **Primary documents** — judgments, legislation, court pleadings, historical documents, institutional resolutions.
2. **Academic scholarship** — peer-reviewed or university-published historical and theological research.
3. **Institutional sources** — IHRA, UCT, museums, archives, government records.
4. **Reputable journalism** — for contemporary chronology and reporting.
5. **Your own published research** — as an interpretive source, clearly identified as such.

The four reference cards currently drawn from the Medialternatives article should be triangulated before publication. The draft itself already recognises this weakness.

## Main editorial recommendation

The deck's strongest conceptual move is **not “these are all claims that are false.”** It is:

> **Learn to recognise the structure of an argument, test the factual proposition independently, and distinguish criticism of a state or ideology from the recycling of a historical anti-Jewish stereotype.**

That principle also gives the deck room to contain difficult cards such as Settler-Colonialism, River to Sea, Weaponizing Antisemitism, IHRA vs Jerusalem Declaration and Depo-Provera without forcing every contested proposition into the same category.
